Postcards · Feature explainer · 09 October 2026

A little of the trip.
A reason to come back.

Give someone one memorable postcard to try. Let them share it freely, forgive a mistake, and make an ongoing postcard habit a reason to subscribe.

Internal product and developer reference. Describes the current branch, not a claim that every change is live.

The product boundary

Be generous with sharing.
Charge for making more.

The subscription boundary is the creation of another postcard. It is not the number of friends who receive a link, the number of times a saved link is shared, or a recipient’s ability to open it.

1 originalA complete free experience.
1 correctionRoom to fix a mistake.
Free resharingFor the life of each link.

01 / Why this feature exists

A useful gesture that can introduce Packaroo.

A postcard is a small, personal thing someone wants to send. Attractive trip artwork and a short message make it worth opening. A hosted link lets it reach people who have never installed Packaroo.

The first free card demonstrates the experience. Easy resharing increases its reach. Subscription becomes relevant when the sender wants to create more postcards for more trips, occasions, or messages.

What we are intentionally avoiding

An unlimited free postcard generator, repeated demo-trip allowances, a paywall for recipients, and elaborate anti-abuse machinery that creates more friction than the occasional extra free card costs.

This is the intended growth path, not a measured conversion result. The recipient footer provides a route to the App Store; see the discovery action below.

02 / The actual experience

Keep the trip at the center.

All images below are unretouched captures of the running app. Select a screenshot to inspect it at full size.

One predictable image rule

Use the trip’s cover, whatever its source: Gallery artwork, the user’s photo, or an existing map cover. There is no surprise detour to select different artwork before composing.

Change Image offers Gallery, Photos, and Adjust Image. Map is not a replacement option. Changing or reframing the postcard does not modify the trip cover.

Keep the composer quiet

Change Image and Flip use text labels. The main toolbar action keeps the paper plane for both creating and resharing a postcard. A spinner replaces it while preparing; the “Postcard ready” status confirms when the link is available. Availability and privacy information sit together beneath the card in portrait. The card size and toolbar stay consistent when the landscape controls are rearranged.

The Video action is removed from the composer. Users share one interactive hosted link.

04 / Deliberate leniency

Forgive ordinary mistakes. Keep repeat creation valuable.

A correction, without a countdown

A typo, an awkward crop, or the wrong image should not make the first experience feel punitive. Reopen the original free postcard and edit it. The next successful replacement uses the one free correction.

The original remains in the list with its original link. The corrected version gets a separate link and its own one-year expiry. Friends holding the first link still see the first version; it is not silently changed.

Additional draft edits are free. The correction is consumed only when the replacement upload returns a validated successful receipt.

What we tell the sender

“This uses your one free correction and creates a new link. Your earlier postcard stays unchanged.”

The confirmation appears before the free replacement is created. We do not make users discover the rule after the action.

No penalty for a failed upload

A failed or rejected upload preserves the applicable allowance and the draft. Service throttling asks the user to wait and retry. Drafting, choosing a photo, and cancelling an image adjustment never consume a postcard.

Accept small leaks

The allowance uses local preferences. Reinstalls, different devices, or tampered local state can bypass it. That tradeoff is intentional at this stage: avoid an obvious unlimited loop, keep the normal path fair, and do not build identity checks merely to prevent an occasional extra card.

The free allowance is used when the link is created

The app explains this before the first free creation. A successful upload makes the postcard available online and uses the allowance. The system share sheet opens next. Copying the link, cancelling, or returning later does not create a second postcard.

There is no “delivered” claim

An upload is not a sent message. The share sheet can copy a link or be cancelled. Packaroo therefore says “Postcard ready,” not “Sent,” and provides no read receipt or delivery confirmation.

Each hosted link remains accessible for one calendar year from creation. Resharing does not renew that year. Cancelling a subscription does not shorten the life of an already-created link.

06 / Make the image work

Adjust the postcard, leave the trip alone.

The original image and chosen framing are kept with the local draft. The app renders a cropped image for the postcard payload so the recipient sees the intended composition. The accompanying web-renderer change prevents a second crop in both the browser and the downloaded front.

Deployment dependency: the web crop-preservation change is present in this repository but has not been deployed or verified on the live host.

07 / Use the available space

Portrait gives it breathing room. Landscape gives it columns.

Portrait

The image controls stay close to the card. Status and privacy text are centered below them, with a 50-point gap. The expiry sits directly below the ready label.

Landscape and text entry

When space allows, the card and a compact controls column sit side by side. The controls column is vertically centered against the card, with closely grouped actions and left-aligned supporting text. Tapping the message opens a separate, readable editor above the keyboard. Larger accessibility text uses a stacked fallback.

08 / The recipient gets the postcard, without a gate

One link across iPhone, Android, and desktop.

The hosted viewer opens in a browser, displays the artwork and message, supports flipping the card, and offers downloads of the front and back. Viewing does not require a Packaroo subscription, sign-in, or installation.

On iPhone this is also the browser experience. This branch does not restore the earlier App Clip or a dedicated native hosted-link recipient flow.

Privacy is part of the promise

Anyone with the link can view the card until it expires. It is not an authenticated, recipient-only message. The composer discloses this before sharing.

Recipients can save images or screenshots. Link expiry cannot remove copies they have already saved.

A little postal personality

The Packaroo Post stamp travels with the message.

The supplied stamp replaces the empty placeholder in the upper-right corner of the back. It appears in the app, browser viewer, and downloadable back image, for both artwork and personal-photo postcards.

09 / The word-of-mouth step

Make the maker easy to discover.

Implemented in the Worker · deployment pending

A quiet attribution and “Get Packaroo” action

The updated recipient page includes a quiet “Made with Packaroo” footer with Apple’s official “Download on the App Store” badge below the viewing controls. The 32-pixel app icon sits above the trip-title heading, which uses Figtree Bold, and links to packaroo.app in a new tab. A small “For iPhone and iPad” label makes platform availability clear.

The action opens the app’s App Store listing in a new tab, keeping the postcard available. It sends no postcard link as a referrer and uses no tracking parameters. Recipients can enjoy the full card without installing anything.

The same web App Store listing works as a product-information destination on Android and desktop. Attribution stays outside the artwork and is not added to the downloaded images.

Six aggregate events, now implemented

Creation is counted after successful hosting, split into free original, free correction, and subscriber creation. The other five events count a visible recipient-page open, a Packaroo-icon click, an App Store-badge click, a postcard-paywall appearance, and a verified production purchase through that paywall.

These counters use the existing Cloudflare Worker and an Analytics Engine dataset. Collection starts after deployment and the app release; this branch does not yet produce a live report.

Do not mistake activity for success

Repeated shares of one link are desirable distribution. Repeated creation of unrelated free cards would weaken the subscription boundary. Review those behaviors separately before tightening restrictions.

Read the counts honestly

Views count page loads, not unique people; reloads and bots can inflate them. An App Store click is not an install. Purchases are counted only after a verified production purchase in the postcard paywall, excluding restores, cancellations, pending approvals, and sandbox purchases. Later approval of a pending purchase is not attributed back to the original paywall.

Only event names, creation category, counts, and timestamps go into the dataset. No postcard content, card URL, trip name, user identifier, or transaction identifier is included. Browser events omit referrers and cookies. Debug builds suppress app reporting. Events are best effort, with no retry queue; blockers, offline use, and rate limits can reduce counts. Client-reported events and categories can be spoofed and never control allowance enforcement.

Review weekly totals using the weekly summary query. Compare discovery clicks with views and purchases with paywall appearances as directional ratios, not person-level conversion rates. Check operational failures and throttling separately. Automated reports and alerts are not configured.

10 / Implementation and release notes

What is here. What still needs attention.

Implemented in this branch

App and recipient experience

  • Per-trip local library, automatic drafts, and preserved shared versions; artwork-led empty state, softer Create Postcard action, and larger saved-card rows.
  • Free original, one free correction, and free reuse of valid saved links.
  • Explicit allowance confirmations and honest ready/expiry feedback.
  • Current-cover default, Gallery/Photos selection, and image reframing.
  • Landscape controls centered beside the card with left-aligned copy; separate message editing and no automatic keyboard opening.
  • Packaroo Post stamp in the composer, hosted back, and saved-back image.
  • Recipient page: linked Packaroo icon, Figtree title, quiet attribution, and official App Store badge for iPhone and iPad.
  • Six aggregate analytics events with no user or postcard identifiers.
Before release

Deployment and follow-through

  • Deploy and verify the web crop-preservation change and Packaroo Post stamp, including the downloaded back.
  • Resolve orientation continuity: this simulator walkthrough returned to the Trips list when rotating a postcard opened from that list. The saved draft survived and reopened.
  • Check portrait, landscape, hardware and software keyboards, large text, and VoiceOver on devices.
  • Deploy and verify the recipient attribution, Get Packaroo action, analytics dataset, and event-rate-limit bindings. Release the updated app and review the privacy disclosures.

Storage and migration limits

Postcards are saved in app-local files; this feature does not sync its history through iCloud. Clearing app data or reinstalling can lose the local list and reset local allowance state. Cards created before this storage feature cannot all be reconstructed: the old implementation retained only the last fingerprint and receipt, not a full history.

The previous receipt can still be reused when matched and is accepted as a legacy correction candidate where applicable. Do not promise recovery of every old postcard.

Verified so far

Simulator app build passed. Seven targeted iOS tests passed for receipt reuse, correction eligibility, failed uploads, stored drafts/versions, and framing bounds. Sixteen Worker tests passed, including hosted expiry, validation, browser safety, preserving framed photo pixels, event validation, and analytics failure isolation.

These are local verification results. No production upload was performed for this explainer. As of October 10, the updated Worker has not been deployed: Cloudflare rejected deployment with Analytics Engine error 10089 despite dataset setup. The previous production version remains active. The existing WebGL bending-flip implementation is preserved in this branch; visually verify it after deployment.

Developer reference: where the behavior lives
  • PostCardSheet.swift — composer, feedback, autosave hooks, and landscape layout.
  • TripPostcardSheet.swift — per-trip saved-card list.
  • PostcardLibrary.swift and SavedPostcard.swift — local persistence and immutable shared versions.
  • PostcardSharingModel.swift — receipt history and the correction rule.
  • PostcardImageAdjustment.swift and PostcardFraming.swift — reframing and rendered crop.
  • worker.mjs — anonymous hosted links, one-year expiry, browser viewer, and service throttles.
  • SavedPostcardRow.swift — artwork-led saved cards and large-text layout.
  • PostcardAnalytics.swift and analytics.mjs — aggregate events and ingestion.
  • weekly-analytics.sql — aggregate report query.
  • Hosting notes — service and verification details.

Creation limits are client-side. The Worker checks payload size, format, expiration, and abuse limits; it does not authenticate a subscription. Do not describe the allowance as tamper-proof or a strict per-person guarantee.
